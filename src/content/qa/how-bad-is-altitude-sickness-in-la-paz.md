## Why La Paz is different

Most visitors fly in from near sea level and land at El Alto, about 4,061 m. The city centre is lower, at about 3,640 m, but still well above the 2,500–3,000 m where altitude sickness becomes likely. Flying in skips the gradual ascent that guidelines recommend.

## What you may feel

Common symptoms of acute mountain sickness are headache, nausea, tiredness, dizziness and poor sleep. Being out of breath on stairs and hills is normal at this height even without illness. Symptoms usually ease over a few days if you do not go higher.

## How to reduce it

- Rest for the first day or two. Avoid hard exercise and alcohol.
- Do not go higher, for example to Chacaltaya or a trek, until you feel well.
- If possible, start your trip lower. Santa Cruz or Sucre in Bolivia, or Cusco in Peru, work well. See [typical routes](/guide/typical-routes/).
- Ask a doctor or travel clinic about acetazolamide before you travel.

## When it is serious

Get help and descend if you notice:

- confusion, unsteadiness or unusual drowsiness (possible HACE);
- breathlessness at rest, or a persistent cough (possible HAPE);
- symptoms that get worse despite rest.

These are medical emergencies. The [altitude ladder](/altitude/) explains the guideline rate of ascent for the rest of your trip.
