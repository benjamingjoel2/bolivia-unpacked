## The main risks

- **Road accidents.** Vehicles drive long distances on salt and unpaved desert tracks, often in convoys. UK government travel advice warns about accidents on salar tours and suggests checking the safety standards of tour companies.
- **Altitude.** The trip climbs from about 3,656 m on the salar to around 4,800–5,000 m at the Sol de Mañana geysers, with nights at about 4,300 m or higher. If you start in Uyuni straight after arriving in Bolivia, or from San Pedro de Atacama in Chile at about 2,400 m, you ascend fast.
- **Cold.** Lodges in Sur Lípez are basic and often unheated, and nights are well below freezing in winter.
- **Remoteness.** Medical care is far away and mobile signal is patchy or absent for much of the route.

## Questions to ask an operator

- How old are the vehicles, and how are they maintained?
- Do drivers work fixed hours, and is drinking forbidden on tour?
- Does each vehicle carry a spare tyre, a first-aid kit, oxygen and a way to call for help where there is no signal?
- How many passengers per vehicle, and where do they sit?
- What happens if someone gets altitude sickness?
- How do they change the route when the salar is flooded?

## Before you go

Spend time above 3,500 m before the trip if you can, for example in La Paz or Potosí. Bring warm layers, a hat, gloves and a sleeping bag rated for freezing nights if the operator does not provide one. Check your insurance covers the heights involved.
