## Why it happens

The Salar de Uyuni is almost perfectly flat. When rain falls in the wet season, a thin sheet of water can sit on the surface. On a calm day, the water reflects the sky and the salt flat looks like a mirror.

## When to go for it

- **Most likely**: January to March in a normal rainy season.
- **Possible**: late December, and early April after a wet year.
- **Unlikely**: May to November, when the salt is dry.

The amount of rain varies from year to year. A dry wet season may give little standing water. Wind also matters, because ripples break the reflection.

## The trade-offs

- **Access**: deep water can stop vehicles reaching Isla Incahuasi or crossing the salar to the south. Operators change routes, and some 3-day trips go round the salar.
- **Weather**: cloudy or stormy days are more common in the wet season.
- **Salt damage**: salty water is hard on shoes, clothes and camera gear.

If you want both the mirror and the full Lípez route, ask your operator how they handle each in the month you plan to go. The [salt-flats month guide](/salt-flats-by-month/) shows the typical pattern month by month.
