Bolivia is in the southern hemisphere and in the tropics, so the year is defined less by temperature than by rain. Most of the country has one wet season and one dry season.

## The pattern

- **Dry season, roughly May to October.** Clear skies are common in the highlands. Days are sunny and nights are cold, coldest from June to August. This is the main season for trekking, climbing and the full Uyuni–Lípez route.
- **Wet season, roughly November or December to March.** Rain falls mostly as afternoon storms in the highlands and as heavy rain in the lowlands. Roads and flights can be disrupted, especially in the Amazon and the Yungas.

Altitude matters as much as the month. In La Paz and on the Altiplano, nights can be near or below freezing much of the year. Santa Cruz and the Amazon are warm to hot all year.

## By region

| Region | Dry season (roughly May–Oct) | Wet season (roughly Dec–Mar) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Salar de Uyuni and Lípez | Dry white salt, full route usually open, very cold nights | Mirror effect possible; water can block routes |
| La Paz, Titicaca | Clear, cold nights | Afternoon storms, milder nights |
| Cordillera Real | Main climbing and trekking season | Fewer climbs; more cloud and snow |
| Yungas | Drier roads | Landslides and road delays possible |
| Amazon (Rurrenabaque) | Wildlife easier to find in the pampas as water shrinks | Heavy rain, flooding, flight and road disruption |
| Santa Cruz, missions | Warm; cold fronts possible | Hot, humid, heavy rain |

## The salt flats mirror

The mirror effect on the Salar de Uyuni needs a thin layer of standing water. It therefore belongs to the wet season and depends on how much rain falls that year.
