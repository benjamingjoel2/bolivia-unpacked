Distances in Bolivia are long and roads cross high passes and steep valleys. Most trips mix domestic flights with road travel.

## Flights

Boliviana de Aviación (BoA), the state airline, flies between the main cities, including La Paz, Santa Cruz, Cochabamba, Sucre, Tarija and Uyuni. Other domestic carriers operate some routes. Rurrenabaque has short flights from La Paz. Domestic flights are the easiest way to save days, for example La Paz to Uyuni or Uyuni to Santa Cruz. Check current routes and timetables with the airlines, as they change.

## Buses

Long-distance buses link almost every town, many of them overnight. Standards vary a lot between companies. The UK government's travel advice warns about road safety in Bolivia. Daytime travel and better-known companies reduce the risk. Road blockades during protests can stop travel for days.

## The Uyuni rail line

Empresa Ferroviaria Andina runs passenger trains on the line from Oruro south through Uyuni and Tupiza to Villazón on the Argentine border. Services run only a few days a week, and the timetable and the condition of the line change. Check the operator's official channels close to your travel date. Do not rely on third-party timetables.

## 4x4 tours

The Salar de Uyuni and Sur Lípez are mainly visited in 4x4 vehicles with a driver, usually on organised tours. See [the Uyuni salt flats and Sur Lípez](/guide/uyuni-and-sur-lipez/).

## In La Paz

The Mi Teleférico cable car lines connect El Alto, the centre and the southern districts.

## Crossing to Peru and Chile

- **Peru**: by road along Lake Titicaca (Kasani–Yunguyo near Copacabana, or Desaguadero), or by air.
- **Chile**: the Uyuni–Lípez tour can end at the Hito Cajón border post for San Pedro de Atacama. There are also road crossings further north, and flights.
