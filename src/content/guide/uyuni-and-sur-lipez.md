The Salar de Uyuni is the largest salt flat in the world, at about 3,656 m in the south-west of Bolivia. South of it, the Sur Lípez region holds high-altitude lagoons, volcanoes and geyser fields, much of it inside the Eduardo Avaroa Andean Fauna National Reserve.

## The standard trip

Most visitors join a 4x4 tour from the town of Uyuni. The common format is three days and two nights:

1. **Day 1**: the train cemetery outside Uyuni, the salt flat itself and, in the dry season, Isla Incahuasi in the middle of the salar.
2. **Day 2**: south through Sur Lípez, past several lagoons, to Laguna Colorada at about 4,278 m.
3. **Day 3**: an early start for the Sol de Mañana geysers, at around 4,800–5,000 m and usually the highest point of the trip, then hot springs and Laguna Verde. From here, trips either return to Uyuni or drop passengers at the Chilean border for San Pedro de Atacama.

Shorter one-day trips cover only the salar. Longer trips start or end in Tupiza.

## Seasons in short

- **Wet season, roughly December to March**: a thin layer of rain can turn the salar into a mirror. Water can also stop vehicles crossing to Isla Incahuasi and the southern route, so itineraries change.
- **Dry season, roughly May to October**: the salt is dry, white and patterned in hexagons, and the full route is normally open. June to August brings very cold nights.

## Safety and comfort

This is a remote trip at high altitude in shared vehicles. UK government travel advice points to road accidents on salar tours and suggests checking the operator's safety standards. See [is the Uyuni 3-day trip safe?](/questions/is-the-uyuni-3-day-trip-safe/) Lodges in Sur Lípez are basic and often unheated. Pack for nights well below freezing.
