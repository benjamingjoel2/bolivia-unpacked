Potosí and Sucre are both on the UNESCO World Heritage list and lie a few hours apart by road. They are often visited together on the way to or from Uyuni.

## Potosí

Potosí grew around the Cerro Rico silver mountain from the 16th century and was one of the largest cities in the Americas in the colonial period. At about 4,090 m it is one of the highest cities in the world, higher than La Paz.

- **Casa Nacional de la Moneda**: the colonial mint, now a museum.
- **Cerro Rico mine visits**: working cooperative mines can be visited with guides. Conditions underground are hot, cramped and dusty, at high altitude. Decide carefully, and go only with an operator who gives proper safety equipment.

## Sucre

Sucre is Bolivia's constitutional capital and seat of the judiciary. At about 2,790 m it is noticeably easier to breathe in than Potosí or La Paz, which makes it a common rest stop. The centre is known for whitewashed colonial buildings.

- **Casa de la Libertad**, where Bolivia's independence was declared.
- **Markets and weaving**: the villages around Sucre, such as Tarabuco, are known for textiles.
- **Dinosaur tracks** at Cal Orck'o on the edge of the city.

## Getting between them

The road between Potosí and Sucre is paved and served by buses and shared taxis. Sucre's airport has domestic flights. See [getting around](/guide/getting-around/).
