Entry rules depend on your nationality and change from time to time. Treat this page as a list of where to check, and check again close to your travel date.

## Visas

- **UK citizens**: UK government advice says British tourists can visit without a visa for up to 90 days in a year. Border officers often stamp 30 days.
- **US citizens**: Bolivia announced that US citizens no longer need a tourist visa from 1 December 2025. Confirm the current rule with the US Department of State and Bolivia's migration authority before you travel, as Bolivia's rules for US citizens have changed several times.
- **Other nationalities**: check Bolivia's Dirección General de Migración and your own government's travel advice.

## Passport and arrival

UK advice says your passport should be valid for at least six months from arrival and have two blank pages, and that you may be asked for proof of accommodation. Make sure your passport is stamped on entry, including at land borders, or you may be fined when you leave.

## Yellow fever

- **Certificate on arrival**: Bolivia may ask for a yellow fever certificate depending on where you are arriving from. UK and US government sources word this differently. Check both before you travel.
- **Vaccine recommendation**: CDC recommends the vaccine for travellers aged 9 months and over going to areas below 2,300 m east of the Andes. That covers all of Beni, Pando and Santa Cruz departments, plus parts of Chuquisaca, Cochabamba, La Paz and Tarija. It does not recommend the vaccine for travel limited to La Paz and Sucre.

This matters for trips to Rurrenabaque, the Yungas lowlands, Santa Cruz and the Jesuit missions.

## Malaria

CDC lists malaria transmission in areas below 2,500 m, but not in the city of La Paz. Ask a travel clinic whether you need tablets for your route.

## Insurance

Check that your travel insurance covers the heights you will reach, for example the 4,800–5,000 m on the Uyuni–Lípez trip or a 6,000 m climb, and covers evacuation.
