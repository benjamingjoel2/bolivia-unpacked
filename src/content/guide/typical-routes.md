These are route outlines, not itineraries. Days include travel. Each one is ordered to reduce the altitude risk set out on the [altitude ladder](/altitude/).

## One week: the highlights

1. La Paz, two or three easy days, with a day trip to Tiwanaku or the city sights.
2. Fly to Uyuni.
3. 3-day salar and Lípez trip, ending in Uyuni, or crossing to San Pedro de Atacama in Chile.

This route sleeps high from the start. It suits people who already know how they handle altitude.

## Two weeks: Bolivia, starting low

1. Santa Cruz or Sucre first (about 400 m or 2,790 m).
2. Sucre to Potosí (about 4,090 m) by road.
3. Potosí to Uyuni by road, then the 3-day salar and Lípez trip.
4. Fly or travel to La Paz. Day trips, then Lake Titicaca and Isla del Sol.

## Two to three weeks: Bolivia and the Amazon

As above, then fly from La Paz to Rurrenabaque for jungle and pampas trips. Ending in the lowlands gives a recovery from altitude before the flight home.

## Bolivia with Peru

Cusco and the Sacred Valley, Machu Picchu, Puno and Lake Titicaca, then Copacabana and La Paz, with the salar added if time allows. See [can I combine Bolivia with Peru in two weeks?](/questions/combine-bolivia-and-peru-in-two-weeks/)

## Bolivia with Chile

The 3-day salar and Lípez trip can end at the Chilean border for San Pedro de Atacama, or run in reverse from there. Both directions are common. Starting from San Pedro (about 2,400 m) means the climb to the Lípez lagoons comes on the first day, which is a fast ascent.

Verified operators running multi-country trips are in the [overland with Peru and Chile ranking](/operators/overland-peru-chile/).
