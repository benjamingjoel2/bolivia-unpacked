La Paz is the seat of Bolivia's government and the place most visitors arrive. Sucre is the constitutional capital. La Paz fills a steep canyon below the flat Altiplano plateau. El Alto, a separate city, sits on the plateau rim above it.

## Height and layout

The city centre is at about 3,640 m. The city falls to around 3,200 m in the southern districts and rises above 4,000 m at the rim. El Alto International Airport is at about 4,061 m, so you land higher than you will sleep.

The cable car network, Mi Teleférico, links El Alto with the centre and the southern districts. It is a practical way to cross the city without climbing streets on foot in your first days.

## What people come for

- **The city itself**: the markets around Calle Sagárnaga and the Witches' Market area, the colonial Calle Jaén, Plaza Murillo and the view from the cable cars.
- **Valle de la Luna**: eroded clay formations on the southern edge of the city, lower than the centre.
- **Tiwanaku**: a UNESCO World Heritage archaeological site on the Altiplano, usually visited as a day trip by road.
- **Chacaltaya and the Cordillera Real**: day trips to over 5,000 m. Do these only after you have acclimatised. See [Cordillera Real](/guide/cordillera-real/).
- **The Yungas road**: the downhill bike trip from La Cumbre to Coroico. See [Yungas](/guide/yungas/).

## First days

Arriving by air takes you straight above 3,500 m. Plan the first two or three days as easy days, avoid alcohol and hard exercise, and do not go higher until you feel well. The [altitude ladder](/altitude/) shows every major stop at its height, with the acclimatisation advice for each step.

If your trip also includes lower places, such as Sucre, the Yungas or the Amazon, consider arranging the route so you reach La Paz after them. See [typical routes](/guide/typical-routes/).
